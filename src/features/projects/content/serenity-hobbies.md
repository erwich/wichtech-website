Serenity Hobbies is a brick-and-mortar hobby and gaming store in Norwich, New York, where I went to high school. Its owner is one of my best friends. He was also my high school math teacher, and years later, he stood in my wedding. So this project has always been personal.

## Helping the shop keep going

When the pandemic hit in 2020, the physical shop had to close. He needed a way for people to browse what he had, place orders, and pay online. The tools he was trying weren’t giving him what he needed, so he reached out to me.

I dropped what I was doing and built him a site with **WordPress and WooCommerce**, then set up **Stripe for integrated payments**. We worked through it together, iterating until it fit the way he needed to run the shop.

The immediate goal was practical: someone could order from home, pay ahead of time, and pick up their purchase curbside. It gave the store a way to keep serving customers while its doors were closed. I like to think I helped him get through a really difficult stretch.

## My part in it

I built the website and online store, connected payments, and worked with him to get the ordering experience right. I’ve continued helping maintain the site since 2020. Every once in a while, we give it a little more love as the shop’s needs change.

That ongoing relationship is a big part of the project. He knows his customers and puts a lot of effort into finding things they want. I help keep the website useful to him and the people shopping there.

## A local shop reaching farther

One of the coolest surprises has been hearing about the calls he gets from people overseas. He tracks down hard-to-find items and lists them in the store, and people as far away as Paris and Germany have found them and called to ask about international shipping.

I’m not going to pretend that was some masterful SEO strategy on my part. He does the work of finding interesting inventory, and the website gives people a way to discover it. Hearing that someone halfway around the world found this little shop in Norwich through something we built together is pretty awesome.

## Still part of the shop

What began as an urgent project during the pandemic has become an ongoing collaboration. The site is still active, and I’m still helping with it six years later.

This is the kind of work I’m proud to have in my portfolio: something useful for someone I care about, with a life well beyond the initial build.
